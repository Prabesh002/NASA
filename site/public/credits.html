Method

  1. For each scene, NASA Harmony clips the three layers to the study area (85.10–85.65°E, 27.85–28.42°N, in two boxes) and returns GeoTIFFs in UTM zone 45N at 10 m (20 m for one radar mode).
  2. Pairs are consecutive passes on the same track, 12 days apart. The later scene is put on the earlier scene’s grid.
  3. Pixels flagged invalid in either scene’s mask are dropped. Backscatter power is averaged in a 5 × 5 pixel window, then change = 10 · log10(after / before), in decibels.
  4. River paths are traced downstream along OpenStreetMap waterway lines. Pixels within 100 m of a path are grouped into 500 m bins by distance downstream. Per bin we report the share of pixels whose change exceeds ±3 dB.
  5. The same statistic is computed for every other 12-day step on track 98 (June to October 2026) and for four neighbouring rivers, as a baseline for ordinary change.
  6. Map tiles are the change rasters resampled to Web Mercator and coloured: blue for a fall, red for a rise, transparent below 1.5 dB, fully opaque from 4 dB.

The step from 26 Jul to 19 Aug is not used: it spans a mission-wide data gap and is 24 days long.

What this is not

This is a hackathon prototype. Radar change shows where the surface changed between two passes. It cannot tell what was damaged. The data are provisional releases. Nothing here is an official assessment, a forecast or a warning.
